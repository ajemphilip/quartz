# Goals
- Bring the cow character back onto the flat casing of step 21
- Let the head be set at more than one angle and be taken off for transport
- Record the small single number adjustments that were made across the printed parts in May 2026

# Description
The first device carried a head and a tail that were pushed into cube shaped holders on the casing (see [[11. Creative Presentation of Device Orientation and Device Casing Adjustments]]). The new top shell keeps those two cube sockets, 10 mm wide, one at the front and one at the back. What changed is the part between the socket and the head. It is now a separate key. One end of the key is a cube that plugs into the socket of the shell. The other end is a round boss with a flat bar across it that enters the back of the head. The back of the head has four slots for that bar at 0, 18, 36 and 48 degrees, so the head can be put on at four angles.

The second half of this step is a list. Between 9 and 19 May 2026 many parts were saved again with one number changed and a name that says which one. They are collected here because together they show how the fits were tuned once everything was being printed in PETG on the Prusa machines recorded in steps 26 to 28.

Reading the dates back, the head was drawn on the evening after the clamp ring evening of step 24. The order of that week is mechanism first, character second, and both within two days. The head itself needed one correction, made 21 minutes after its first export, and was then left alone. The key is where the time went. Its cube end was 9.5 mm in April, 9 mm late on 11 May and 9.9 mm just after midnight, all for a socket of 10 mm. The fit was approached from both sides within an hour and a half, and the grip dots of the socket were halved in the same minute that the last key was saved. One reading is that the cube and the grip dots were fighting each other, and that the fit only settled once both were changed together.

# Critical Reflection
## The key as a separate part
**Pros**
- The part that takes the wear, the cube in its socket, is small and quick to print again, while the large head stays untouched
- Four angles let the head look up at a child across a table or sit level on a shelf
- The head comes off for transport and the device still closes

**Cons**
- The head hangs on a bar of 4.3 mm in a slot of 5 mm, so it may rock a little at every angle
- A cube that went from 9.5 to 9 to 9.9 mm shows that the fit depends on the printer and the material. Another printer may need another number

## Tuning by file name
The adjustment table shows a way of working that is easy to miss when looking at the final files only. Almost every change is one number, and the old file is kept beside the new one. That costs disk space and leaves a folder with more than 120 exports, but it also means that the path to each final value can still be read months later, which is what made this workbook possible.
