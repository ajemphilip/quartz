# Goals
- Bring the cow character back onto the flat casing of step 21
- Let the head be put on and taken off by hand, without tools, so the device can travel without it
- Record the small single number adjustments that were made across the printed parts in May 2026

# Description
The first device carried a head and a tail that were pushed into cube shaped holders on the casing (see [[11. Creative Presentation of Device Orientation and Device Casing Adjustments]]). The new top shell keeps those two cube sockets, 10 mm wide, one at the front and one at the back. What changed is the part between the socket and the head. It is now a separate key. One end of the key is a cube that plugs into the socket of the shell. The other end is a round boss with a flat bar across its tip. The back of the head has a round hole with one slot, like a keyhole. The bar goes in through the slot, the head is turned by 45 degrees, and the two ends of the bar snap onto two small pins inside the head. It is a twist lock with one locked position.

The second half of this step is a list. Between 5 and 19 May 2026 many parts were saved again with one number changed and a name that says which one. They are collected here because together they show how the fits were tuned once everything was being printed in PETG on the Prusa machines recorded in steps 26 to 28.

Reading the dates back, the head was drawn on the evening after the clamp ring evening of step 24. The order of that week is mechanism first, character second, and both within two days. The head itself needed one correction, made 21 minutes after its first export, and was then left alone. The key is where the time went. Its cube end was 9.5 mm in April, 8.7 mm and then 9 mm late on 11 May, and 9.9 mm just after midnight, all for a socket of 10 mm. The fit was approached from both sides within an hour and a half, and the grip dots of the socket were halved in the same minute that the last key was saved. One reading is that the cube and the grip dots were fighting each other, and that the fit only settled once both were changed together.

| Locking pins | Two, radius 1 mm, later 0.85 mm, in the chamber 15.6 mm from the centre, on opposite sides at 45 degrees from the slot |

| Key cube | 25 mm long, tilted 40 degrees against the bar. 9.5 mm wide, then 8.7, then 9 and finally 9.9 mm |
| Socket on the shell | Cube of 10 mm at the front and at the back of the top shell, with a 2 mm support wall and four grip dots |

## How the lock was checked
The key was turned inside the head in OpenSCAD and the overlap of the two parts was measured at each angle. From 0 to 36 degrees the parts do not touch. Between 40 and 44 degrees they overlap by about 2 cubic millimetres, which is the bar riding over the two pins. At 45 degrees the overlap is gone, because the pins sit in the notches. Past 48 degrees the bar runs into the end of the chamber, and turned the other way it is blocked at once. The four slot angles in the source are therefore not four positions of the head. They are four cuts that together make the room for one turn.

Seated, the back of the head lies flat against the front wall of the top shell and the boss of the key is hidden inside the head. The cube is tilted by 40 degrees and the lock sits at 45 degrees, so as modelled the head ends up 5 degrees away from level.

# Critical Reflection
## The key as a separate part
**Pros**
- The part that takes the wear, the cube in its socket, is small and quick to print again, while the large head stays untouched
- The head goes on and comes off with a turn of 45 degrees. No tool and no screw is needed
- The head comes off for transport and the device still closes

**Cons**
- The head hangs on a bar of 4.3 mm in a chamber of 5 mm and is held by two pins of 0.85 mm, so it may rock a little
- As modelled the head sits 5 degrees off level, because the cube is tilted by 40 degrees and the lock is at 45
- A cube that went from 9.5 to 8.7 to 9 to 9.9 mm shows that the fit depends on the printer and the material. Another printer may need another number

## Tuning by file name
The adjustment table shows a way of working that is easy to miss when looking at the final files only. Almost every change is one number, and the old file is kept beside the new one. That costs disk space and leaves a folder with more than 120 exports, but it also means that the path to each final value can still be read months later, which is what made this workbook possible.
