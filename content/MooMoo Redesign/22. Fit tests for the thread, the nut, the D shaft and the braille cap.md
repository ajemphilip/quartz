# Goals
- Find a thread pitch that a desktop printer can produce and that a small geared motor can turn
- Find the clearance between the printed nut and the printed screw
- Find the size of the D shaped hole that grips the motor shaft
- Check the seat between the braille cap and the post it sits on, without printing a whole cap each time

# Description
The first lead screw of step 20 had a pitch of 1.5 mm, a nut clearance of 0.05 mm and a D shaft hole of radius 1.05 mm. None of these three numbers survived. This step covers the eight days at the end of January and the start of February 2026 in which each of them was isolated into its own small part and printed separately. The parts are only a few millimetres in size, so each test costs minutes of printing and not hours.

The values that came out of this step can be read in `shaft mechanism.scad` as saved on 28 March 2026. They are compared with the December values in the table further down.

The order of the files tells the story of the day. A larger pitch is tried before noon. In the evening the nut and the elevator are made taller, then the pitch is brought back down, and only then is the D shaft hole cut out as a disc 2.8 mm thick, one of the smallest parts in the folder. Two test rods were exported on 3 February, one with a pitch of 4.5 mm and one with a pitch of 3 mm. The file of 28 March keeps 4.5 mm. Step 26 shows that the choice was reversed in May and that the final screws use 3 mm.

## The braille cap seat
The cap of this step is a cup of 40 mm diameter with a post of 9.6 mm diameter hanging from its roof. The post ends in a recess of 5.6 mm diameter and 6.5 mm depth with four small bumps of radius 0.7 mm inside it. `attachementTestBrailleCap.stl` is that post alone, so the recess and its bumps could be tried on the part below without printing the cup around it. Step 27 shows the final cap and the piston post that it sits on.

# Critical Reflection
## Printing one feature at a time
**Pros**
- A disc with a D shaped hole prints in a few minutes, so several hole sizes can be tried in the time one full screw takes
- A failed test does not throw away a whole actuator body, which by this point was 78.5 mm tall and the slowest part of the module to print
- The test parts stay in the folder as a record of what was tried, even where no notes were written on the day

**Cons**
- The exports have no SCAD source of their own. The values that were tried can only be recovered by measuring the meshes, which is how the pitches in this step were found
- A thread, a nut clearance and a shaft hole that each work alone can still bind once they are stacked in one actuator. The ten screw exports of 12 May 2026 in step 26 show that the screw still needed work once it sat in the full actuator

## Pitch as a trade between speed and force
A larger pitch moves the elevator further for each turn of the motor, so the dot rises faster, but the motor has to push harder for the same load. A smaller pitch does the reverse and also gives thinner thread flanks that are harder to print cleanly. The files show both directions being tried on the same day.
