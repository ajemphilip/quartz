# How it works
## What a phone browser can hold
A search of what browser and library makers have written settled the limit of step 35. On an iPhone a web page is closed at about 1.5 GB, with a hard stop at 2 GB, and the memory of the graphics chip counts towards it. WebKit engineers said so in 2024, the WebKit source says so in October 2026, and other developers' logs show the same stop on an iPhone 16 Pro Max and an iPhone 17 Pro with iOS 26 and 27. No setting raises it, and Chrome on the iPhone uses the same engine as Safari.

The search also found a likely cause of the crashes of step 35 that has nothing to do with the size of a model. The build of the runtime that transformers.js 3.7.5 carries for Whisper and Kokoro makes Safari on the iPhone use gigabytes of memory for a few seconds while it compiles. A WebKit report of September 2026 has an iPhone 16 Pro killed in four of four tries with that build and in none of three with the plain build.

## Kitten TTS 2 in a browser
The voice the maker ranked third on 21 November, Kitten TTS 2 Nano, turned out to be a single file of 17 MB in a form a browser can run. Its English needs no espeak, only a dictionary and a small model for unknown words, which can be worked out before the text reaches the voice. A test page ran it in a browser on the Mac on the processor alone, one thread, with the plain build of the runtime.

| Model | File | Time for a story part of about 9 s | Memory in the page |
|---|---|---|---|
| Kitten 2 Pico | 8 MB | 0.7 s | 69 MB |
| Kitten 2 Nano | 17 MB | 1.4 s | about 160 MB |
| Kitten 2 Mini | 83 MB | 10 s | 315 MB |

Safari on the Mac gave the same times. Every recording was written back word for word by Whisper and sounded as the Kitten 2 that Moo Local runs. It was not tried on the iPhone, because the app took over.

## What the Pi can carry
The maker asked whether the Pi could take some of the models, so that a smaller device only has to run the rest. The Moo's Pi is a Raspberry Pi Zero 2 W with 416 MB of memory, of which about 190 MB are free while the Moo runs. The two small Kitten 2 voices were timed on it in a test folder of their own, `~/moo-bench`.

| Model | Time for a story part | Speech made | Memory |
|---|---|---|---|
| Kitten 2 Pico | 6.8 s | 8.5 s | 176 MB |
| Kitten 2 Nano | 14 to 19 s | 8.9 s | 247 MB, more than is free |

Pico speaks a little faster than it is spoken but leaves almost nothing free. Nano is too slow. No story model that did well in step 32 fits, since the smallest of them needs about ten times the free memory. Moving the voice to the Pi would have saved a phone about 160 MB at the price of the weaker Pico voice and a full Pi, so the maker chose one device for all three jobs.

## One app for every phone
The app started on 21 November for the iPhone, written in React Native with Expo, became one app for iPhone and Android. Its AI library, react-native-executorch, offers each model in several forms and picks the right one for the phone when asked for the default: Core ML or MLX on an iPhone, XNNPACK on Android. The app reads the phone's memory and offers only the models that fit.

| Phone memory | Story model | Listening | Voice |
|---|---|---|---|
| Under 3 GB | LFM2.5 350M | Whisper tiny | Kokoro |
| 3 to 4 GB | LFM2.5 1.2B | Whisper tiny | Kokoro |
| 4 to 6 GB | LFM2.5 1.2B | Whisper base | Kokoro |
| 6 GB and more | Llama 3.2 3B | Whisper base | Kokoro |

Kokoro, which closed the page in every form in step 35, runs in the app on every phone, because an installed app is not held to the limit of a web page. The voice model can also be Supertonic 3, with ten voices, or Reverb, with narrator voices, on phones of 8 GB and more.

Xcode was found installed on the Mac, with the maker's free Apple ID already signed in, so the iPhone app is built and signed on the Mac and installed over the cable. An app signed this way runs for seven days. For Android the maker chose a build in Expo's cloud, and then asked for the tools on the Mac as well. Both work. The Android app file is 54 MB, too large to send to a phone through the chat, and has not yet been put on an Android phone.

## The app as the Moo's remote
The maker asked for an app that controls the Moo rather than one that only lends the phone. The stories and games stay on the Moo, so the app, the website and any computer share them, and the child still talks to the Moo and hears it.

| Tab | What it does |
|---|---|
| Story | Start, continue and stop a story, with what Moo is doing, what it said, and the story so far |
| Games | The four word games of step 32 |
| Letters | Tap a letter and Moo raises its dots. No AI is needed |
| This phone | Who does the Moo's AI, the models of this phone and the Moo's address |

The app finds the Moo by itself on the wifi and starts working for it as soon as it has loaded its models. The first time took about nine minutes on the iPhone 16 Pro, most of it the downloads. At 20:20 the maker started the first story from the app and found that it works well.

## Five faults found in the first stories
| What the maker heard | What the log showed | What changed |
|---|---|---|
| The story intro played twice | The app sent two starts in the same second, and both were taken, so two stories ran at once | The Moo takes one start at a time, and the app sends nothing while a request is on its way. Two starts sent together in a test: one taken, one refused |
| The story went wrong from the first part | The theme was "(beeping) - What should our story be about? (beeping) (buzzing) (buzzing)". The second story's microphone had recorded the first story's question | The same fix. The app also removes sound labels and loops from what Whisper wrote |
| Moo did not hear the child | The noise floor was measured while the chime still rang in the case, once at 0.34, which set the threshold ten times too high | The floor is the quietest moment 0.3 to 0.8 s after the chime |
| Words came from silence | With no speech in 12 seconds the recording was still written out, and Whisper made words of the noise | No speech means an empty answer, without writing anything out |
| The lent device did not answer in time | A copy of the app closed by a new install still had a request waiting on the Moo. It took the story's first job and was gone | A device saying hello again replaces its old entry at once, and the old entry can no longer take a job |

The maker then found that capital letters do not work with the voice. The story model on the phone had copied the capitals of Moo's own instructions, such as SAY OUT LOUD, into what Moo says. Words in capitals are now spoken as ordinary words before any voice reads them. A single letter such as the C of the letter game, and "I", stay as they are, and OK is read as okay.

## The current device and the others
The maker asked for the device in use to stay the current one, with the others listed below it to connect. The Moo now keeps one lent phone or computer as the current device and gives it every job. The others wait until they are chosen. If the current one is not lending, any lent device does the work, so a story never waits on a phone that was put away. The choice is kept by the device's name, so it outlasts a reconnect. A device that stops lending leaves the list at once instead of after 40 seconds. The list is on the AI engine screen of the website and under This phone in the app, where an Add a device button shows three ways to add one: another phone with the app, any browser with the Moo's address, and a computer with Moo Local, each with a button to send it to that device.

# Critical Reflection
## One app on the phone
**Pros**
- One device does the whole AI, with Kokoro and a 3 billion parameter story model on the iPhone, which a web page could not hold
- The same code serves iPhone and Android, and each phone gets the models that fit it
- The app is the Moo's remote, so a parent does not need the website to start a story
- The faults of the first stories were found in the Moo's log, not guessed, and four of them were faults of the Moo itself that every engine shared

**Cons**
- The story model on the phone rejected an answer that the same model on the Mac accepted twelve times out of twelve, and the safety check on the phone flagged a story about a puppy. Why the phone answers differently is not yet known. Its replies are now logged on the Moo
- The app has to stay open while the child plays, and an iPhone app signed with a free Apple ID stops after seven days
- The first start downloads several gigabytes
- Android has not been tried on a phone, and the cloud build of Android is from before the tabs
- The Mac's disk is nearly full after the Android tools and the builds

## In the maker's words
Asked how the app compares with the browser version, the maker answered that the app is more robust for running voice models and AI on a lower end device such as a phone. He found it interesting that the browser cannot keep up and is restricted, while the app has full access to the phone's hardware and can use its memory well. It is fast as well. The iPhone 16 Pro may be a good phone for AI, but the models loaded quickly and the quality of the AI on the phone is not bad, which he finds nice for building this technology locally.

He sees a way to make the electronics simpler and the device cheaper. An ESP32 that connects to the wifi would be the controller, and the phone would do the AI and the work behind the Moo, play the stories and capture the child's answers through its own microphone. The device itself would then be only the braille pins: an ESP32, six motors, the TB6612 motor drivers, six buttons and the printed parts, about 20 dollars less than now.

Asked how the stories and the voice sounded when the phone did the work, he answered that on the Mac it was a bit better, because the Mac has a good sound system and the sound of the Moo is not that great. At the same time the voice was very clear and sounded very nice. He did not find the difference very noticeable, especially for a DIY device, where one expects it to sound a bit worse or about as good as what one pays for. He found it good.

Asked about the story on the phone, which had rejected good answers, he called it a bug. The way the story was built was confusing for the local models and for how it was implemented, so the model did not follow the steps of the design in the implementation.

Asked about the list of devices with the current one at the top and the Add a device button, he answered that it is what he had in mind and pretty good for now, and asked for all the voice models to be added to the app.
