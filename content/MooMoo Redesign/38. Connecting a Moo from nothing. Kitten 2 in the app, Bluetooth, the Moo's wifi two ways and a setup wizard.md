# How it works
## Kitten 2 in the app
Of the voice models timed on the Mac in step 32, the app's AI library carries Kokoro, Supertonic 3 and Reverb. Piper needs espeak, a native library; Pocket exists for phones only in unofficial ports of a model in several parts; and Qwen3, Orpheus and Chatterbox are too large for a phone. Kitten 2 could be added, because step 37 had rewritten its text side in plain JavaScript.

The first plan was to run Kitten 2 on the ONNX runtime for React Native. Its iPhone part is fetched at build time from `download.onnxruntime.ai`, and that name pointed at a retired Azure CDN that answered with a certificate for `*.azureedge.net`. The file was not there under the CDN's own name either, and Microsoft's onnxruntime repository answered Not Found on GitHub. Turning the certificate check off would not have been safe.

Kitten 2 now runs in a web view that the app keeps out of sight. The web view loads the published library of step 37, `kitten-tts-2-browser` v0.1.0, from jsDelivr, as the browser demo does, and the app hands it a line and gets the wav back. A web view is a process of its own with its own memory, so the voice does not take memory from the story model, and the regular expressions and BigInt the library relies on are those of Safari's engine, which has them. The voice model list in the app gained Kitten 2 Pico, Nano and Mini with their own voices: four in Pico, ten in Nano and eleven in Mini.

## The Moo over Bluetooth
The Moo has had a Bluetooth bridge since step 30, `moo-bluetooth`, advertised as Moo-7331. It carries the website's requests in pieces of 20 bytes, up to 32 KB a message, and passes them to the setup app. The app now speaks the same protocol. That is enough for every control and for the wifi, but a sound file of 100 to 500 KB would take about half a minute, so the AI work stays on the wifi. The maker chose that split. To move to the wifi by itself, the app needs the Moo's address, and the Moo's status now gives it, for example 192.168.2.52.

How the app cuts a request into 20 byte pieces, from `phone_app/src/ble.ts`. Each piece starts with 6 bytes: the number of the request, the number of the piece and how many pieces there are. The bridge on the Moo puts them together again.

```ts
for (let i = 0; i < total; i++) {
  const piece = bytes.subarray(i * PAYLOAD, (i + 1) * PAYLOAD);
  const frame = new Uint8Array(6 + piece.length);
  const v = new DataView(frame.buffer);
  v.setUint16(0, id, true);     // which request
  v.setUint16(2, i, true);      // which piece
  v.setUint16(4, total, true);  // of how many
  frame.set(piece, 6);
  await d.writeCharacteristicWithResponseForService(SERVICE, REQUEST, toB64(frame));
}
```

## The Moo's wifi two ways
| Way | What happens |
|---|---|
| Over Bluetooth | The app finds the Moo nearby, reads the networks the Moo can see, and sends the chosen one with its password. The Bluetooth link stays while the Moo changes wifi, so the app waits until the Moo is on it and then moves there |
| Through the Moo's own wifi | A Moo on no wifi makes its own, named moo- and four digits, with no password. The phone joins it, the app finds the Moo at 10.41.0.1 and asks for the home wifi at once. The Moo then leaves its own wifi, and the app looks for it on the home wifi |

Put the Moo on another wifi is also there at any time, from either link. Typing the Moo's address still works.

## From nothing
With the maker's word the Moo was made to forget BELL080 with `comitup-cli d`, the same command the Moo's own reset uses for the wifi. Its calibration and settings stayed. The iPhone found it over Bluetooth, paired after asking the maker, read the wifi networks and gave it BELL080. About four minutes after it had forgotten it, the Moo was on BELL080 again at the same address, and the iPhone was its current device for the AI once more. The maker said it works.

## The wizard
The maker then asked for a connection wizard. Set up a Moo, step by step, in the app, has five steps.

| Step | What it asks |
|---|---|
| 1 | How shall the phone find the Moo: with Bluetooth, the easiest, through the Moo's own wifi, or it is already on my wifi |
| 2 | Find it: a Bluetooth search, or how to join the Moo's own wifi with a button to the phone's Settings, or the Moo's address |
| 3 | Give the Moo your wifi: choose the home wifi and type its password |
| 4 | Put the phone on the same wifi, if the Moo is on another than the phone |
| 5 | The Moo is connected, and Start a story once the phone's models are ready |

The wizard shows the next step from what is true at the moment, so it can be opened at any point.

## Without the app
Asked how it works without the app, the answer was that everything goes through the Moo's website. The Moo's own wifi works from any phone or computer. Bluetooth works only in Chrome or Edge, on a computer or an Android phone, because no browser on an iPhone lets a web page use Bluetooth. Afterwards the Moo is at moo.local on iPhones, iPads and Macs, while many Android phones and some computers need the number address. The AI then runs in the browser, on a computer with Moo Local, or in the cloud.

The website already had a setup in six steps: Connect, Wifi, AI engine, Preferences, Sound check and Done. Rather than a second wizard, it was brought up to the app. Connect gained It is already on my wifi. A page on GitHub Pages is served over https and may not talk to the Moo's plain http address, so from there the Moo's own wifi leads to the Moo's own Connect screen, and after the Moo joins the wifi over Bluetooth the page shows the Moo's address and continues the setup on the Moo. Every handoff says that the device has to be on the same wifi as the Moo. The website was already published to GitHub Pages on every push, at https://ajemphilip.github.io/moo-moo-dashboard/, and the build of `76a0dd9` put the new setup there.

## Downloading the app
Asked how one downloads the app, the answer was that nobody could yet: the app existed only as builds made on the Mac. An iPhone app signed with a free Apple ID installs only from the Mac and stops after seven days. Handing it out needs Apple's developer program, 99 dollars a year, and then TestFlight or the App Store. Android is easier. The Android app was built in Expo's cloud, which keeps one fixed signing key so that later versions install as updates, and published as a release of the Pi code repository, `app-2026-11-22`. The file is 141 MB because it carries the code for every kind of Android phone.

The Moo website, on the Moo and on GitHub Pages, gained a card named Get the Moo app on its start page and dashboard, with Download for Android. The link always points at the newest release. For the iPhone the card says that the app is coming and that the website works on the iPhone until then.

## This device from GitHub Pages
The maker found that This device could not be chosen on GitHub Pages. The page sent the browser to the Moo's secure address but built that address from its own host name, so it opened github.io:8443, which does not exist. It now opens the Moo's own address: the one the Moo gave over Bluetooth, else moo.local. The maker then met the warning about the Moo's certificate. The Moo makes its own certificate, so every browser warns once. Safari and Chrome let a person continue past it, but the browser built into the Claude app does not. A warning-free secure address would need a real certificate for the Moo, a project of its own.

## A blank screen after an update
On the iPhone in Safari, Set up a new Moo on GitHub Pages showed a blank screen. Opened in WebKit, Safari's engine, set up as an iPhone, the published version showed the Connect step, so the published code worked. The likely cause was timing. GitHub Pages lets a browser keep a page for ten minutes, the day's pushes replaced the website's code files, and a start page kept from before pointed at files that no longer existed. When a code file fails to load, the website now reloads itself, at most once a minute, which fetches the new version.

## A local machine without a Terminal
The maker asked for an easier way to set up a local machine than the Terminal line of step 34, on Windows, Mac and Linux. Moo Local's story part already runs on all three through Ollama, but its listening and three of its voices use Apple's MLX and run only on Macs with Apple chips. Making it run everywhere, with an installer to double-click for each system, was started in a session of its own and is not part of this step.

## One list of devices
The maker asked whether a device he sets up is saved to be used later. It was not: the Moo knew only the devices lending at that moment, and remembered just the one computer chosen last. Now the Moo keeps every phone, browser and computer that has worked for it, in its configuration under `known_devices`, with its kind, when it was first and last seen and its address on the home network. A device that is off stays on the list, greyed out with when it was last seen and what to do to bring it back, and Forget takes it off.

The maker then found three entries for one computer: Moo Local on Mac, a second Moo Local on the same Mac and the Mac's browser. The list now has one row per machine, grouped by its address. The row shows the way the machine is used now, or else the best way that is on, and the one in use is highlighted. The Moo also tells a page its own address, so the page marks its row as this device.

This device stopped being a separate choice. Below the list there is one button, Set up this device, and it decides by itself. A computer that runs Moo Local uses it, anything else does the work in the browser, and a computer without Moo Local gets a link to install it. In the app, This phone already stands in the same list. At the maker's request the dashboard lost its Preferences card, and the Moo's status moved into a Settings card with Preferences, Restart and Factory reset.

## Choosing the models
The maker asked that every device offers its models without installing them, and that a model too big for the device says so. The phone app no longer downloads anything at its first start. It shows Choose the models for this phone, with the story model, listening model and voice that suit the phone already chosen and the total download, and fetches them only when Install and start is tapped. Every model is listed; one too big for the phone is greyed out and says why when tapped, and a model that runs out of memory while loading says to choose a smaller one. The same for Moo Local on a computer was built by the session of step 40: one dropdown each for story, listening and voice, where the one selected is the one in use, installed on demand and refused when it does not fit.

## A computer that answered slowly
After Moo Local was installed on the maker's Windows computer, JANUSZ, the AI engine screen showed it as off. The Moo did find it on the network, but its answer to the Moo's health check took 3.3 to 3.7 seconds, and the Moo gave each computer 3 seconds. The Moo now gives each computer 8 seconds and asks them all at the same time, so its search still takes about 4 seconds, and the session of step 40 made Moo Local answer in a few milliseconds.

# Critical Reflection
## Connecting from nothing
**Pros**
- A Moo connected to nothing reached the home wifi from the phone alone, with no address typed
- Two ways to give the Moo its wifi, one of them without Bluetooth, and a wizard that leads through either
- Kitten 2 joined the app without a native library, and in a process of its own

**Cons**
- The AI work cannot go over Bluetooth, so the phone still has to be on the Moo's wifi for stories
- The Moo took about four minutes to come back on the wifi
- On an iPhone without the app, a web page cannot use Bluetooth, so only the Moo's own wifi works there
- Kitten 2 in the app depends on jsDelivr and Hugging Face for its first download
- Installing Moo Local and setting the Moo's Bluetooth setup code still need a Terminal
- The white screen came from an order of definitions that the typecheck did not catch. Only a start on the phone found it
- The iPhone app cannot be handed out without Apple's paid developer program
- This device still meets a certificate warning once on every browser
- A device remembered before addresses were kept shows as its own row until it says hello again
- The Moo's AI was found set to the empty Moo Local on the Mac after a tap in the list, so a story would have failed until the iPhone was chosen again

## In the maker's words
The maker found connecting a Moo from nothing much the same as before: a parent either joins the Moo's wifi or installs the app, and the app is the new way. Apart from that he found it straightforward, and once the Moo is connected the setup wizard is easy to run. He finds the wizard clean, both for people who know the steps and for people who follow written instructions to set the Moo up themselves.

The one problem he sees is on the computer. Moo Local needs Ollama installed, and Ollama is installed by a script that opens a console. He sees this as a more advanced, computer science step, and a parent who sees a console open after a download may worry that they have downloaded a virus.

He found Kitten 2 in the app pretty good. The voice was very clear and lively, and he called it a very good source of voice.
